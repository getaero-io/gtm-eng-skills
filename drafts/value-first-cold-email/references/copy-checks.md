# Copy checks

Run on every draft, touch 1 and every follow-up, before anything is shown. Any hit is a rewrite, not
a note, and a draft does not pass with a known hit in it. Do not narrate the checks, do not say they
ran clean, and do not print this list: fix the draft and deliver it.

**The installer's brand rules win where they conflict with this file**, and the conflict is said out
loud rather than resolved silently.

This list ages fast. Words on it will read as normal within a year and new ones will take their
place, so treat it as a starting set.

## 1. Self-edit checklist

- Would the recipient know in 3 seconds why this is about them?
- Is there a real why-now, from a checked or supplied signal - or did the draft fake relevance?
- Is the pain in their words, not the product's words?
- Did they get something valuable in the email itself, not a promise of it on a call?
- If they read it and did nothing, was it still worth their time?
- Is every piece of value or proof specific, real, and traceable to the brief or the evidence?
- Is there exactly one ask, and is it easy to say yes to?
- Do sentences about them outnumber sentences about you?
- Inside the word range? Scannable on a phone? Link-light?
- Every buzzword, hedge and "just" removed?
- Would you reply to this?

## 2. Literal string pass

Search the draft for each of these. Search, do not skim.

delve, tapestry, testament to, landscape, realm, navigate the complexities, unlock, unleash,
elevate, harness, seamless, robust, game-changer, revolutionize, transformative, at the end of the
day, the truth is, spoiler alert, here's the thing, let's dive in, moreover, furthermore, in today's
fast-paced world, it's worth noting, needless to say, that being said, I've seen firsthand, as
someone who, buckle up, the reality is, make no mistake, it's a mindset, this is where the magic
happens, no fluff

Cold-email filler, same standing:

hope you're well, I came across your profile, I wanted to reach out, just checking in, circling
back, just bumping this, quick question, touching base, I have some ideas to share

Also a hit:

- "honest" or "honestly" in any form;
- "quietly" used as a figure of speech ("quietly became") rather than for sound;
- "It's not X, it's Y" and every variant of it.

## 3. House defaults (the author's; the installer's brand rules replace them)

- No em dashes. Use a hyphen with spaces, or restructure the sentence.
- No bold, no emoji, no images in the email body.
- One CTA, phrased as a question.

## 4. Structural pass

Read only for shape. These give a draft away faster than any single word, and they survive a
find-and-replace, which is why they matter more than the lists above.

- every paragraph the same length;
- a balanced list of three, every time;
- a closing line that restates the email;
- hedging until nothing is actually claimed;
- explaining the point right after making it;
- symmetrical sentence pairs stacked back to back;
- transitions doing work a line break would do better.

The fix for all of them is asymmetry. Real writing is lumpy: one paragraph runs two lines, the next
is four words.

## 5. Opener pass

Check the subject and the first two lines against these, and flag on sight:

"Here's what nobody tells you" · "Let that sink in" · "Read that again" · "Unpopular opinion:" ·
"Hot take:" · "I'll say the quiet part out loud" · "Plot twist:" · any line announcing that a list
follows · any opener that is a compliment with no signal behind it.

# Campaign brief

The intake that turns an offer into cold email worth sending. Fill it in with the person who owns
the offer - a call works better than a form - before any copy exists. The most valuable answers are
in blocks 3, 4 and 5: the buyer's own words, the value you can give them for free, and your real
proof. Generic copy almost always traces back to skipping those, and an email with nothing to give
traces back to skipping the value question.

Capture answers verbatim where possible, especially quotes, and do not pad gaps with guesses. "We
don't know yet" is a finding, not a failure: it says what to validate before sending at volume.

## Minimum viable set (if you only get 5 minutes)

1. The persona (block 2)
2. The problem in their words (block 3)
3. One thing you can give them for free in email one (block 4)
4. One real proof point with a number (block 5)
5. The why-now triggers (block 6)

With those five, plus whether the audience is cold or warm (block 8), a value-first first touch can
be written. Everything else makes it sharper and the sequence deeper.

## 1. Company and offer

- In one sentence, what do you sell and who is it for?
- What does the buyer actually get - the outcome, not the feature list?
- What is the offer in this campaign? (a call, a demo, a free audit, an intro, a pilot, content)
- What is the price band or deal size, and is this a single-buyer or committee sale?

## 2. Who we're targeting (ICP and personas)

- Describe the ideal account: industry, size, region, and any disqualifiers.
- What tells you an account is a strong fit versus a waste of time?
- List each persona. For each:
  - Title(s), and where they sit in the buying process (champion, economic buyer, blocker, end user).
  - What they are measured on, and what makes them look good internally.
  - How much they already understand the problem you solve (unaware to most aware).

## 3. The problem, in the buyer's words - don't skip this

- What painful, expensive or annoying problem do you remove? Be concrete.
- How do buyers describe it in their own words? Paste real quotes from calls, reviews, support tickets or sales notes.
- What are they doing about it today - the status quo or workaround you replace?
- What does doing nothing cost them? (time, money, risk, missed revenue)
- What is the trigger moment when the pain becomes urgent enough to act?

## 4. Outcomes and the value you can give - don't skip this

- What measurable result do customers get? A number or a range, if you have one.
- How fast do they see it?
- What is the "after" state in the buyer's words?
- What can you give this reader for free in the first email - something useful even if they never reply? Pick what you can make most specific:
  - An insight: a pattern or shift you see across companies like theirs that they may not have connected to their own situation.
  - An observation about them: something you can actually notice that hands them a useful read on their own situation.
  - A benchmark: a real number they can measure themselves against. Only if it is true.
  - A resource: a teardown, template, checklist or short guide you can give ungated.
  - A mini-audit: a small piece of the work already done for them.
- Could you do a tiny piece of the work up front, to prove competence and give a gift in one move? What would that look like at scale?

## 5. Proof and differentiation - don't skip this

- Name 2 or 3 comparable customers you can reference, by name or as "a company like X".
- Your best concrete result or case study, with the number.
- Why you over the obvious alternative? One sharp line.
- Any credibility you can borrow: investors, partners, press, certifications, notable team background.

## 6. Why-now triggers

- What events make outreach timely for this buyer?
- Map each one to a check this skill runs, or mark it as not looked up:

| Trigger | Check | What to name |
|---|---|---|
| a new leader in the persona's seat | new in role | the persona titles that count |
| a team being built or grown | hiring | the departments that count |
| a fresh round | funding | nothing extra |
| a launch, expansion, partnership or other public event | company news | the topics that count |
| anything else (a conference, content they posted, a tech install, a regulation) | none | only usable if you supply it as a column in the list; it is marked as not checked |

- How old can a signal be and still be a reason to write today?
- Any time-sensitive hook for this campaign? (an event, a deadline, a season)

## 7. Objections and landmines

- The top 3 reasons buyers say no or go silent.
- What do prospects often assume about you that is not true?
- Any topics, claims or phrasings you must avoid? (legal, brand, compliance, competitor jabs)

## 8. Sender and sending constraints

- Who is the email from? (a founder, an AE, a name on a warmed domain)
- Is this audience cold, warm or 1st-degree, or referred? This changes the whole tone.
- How many sends, and from which sequencer and sending setup?
- Deliverability limits: domain warmth, daily sending caps, link and image rules. First touches should be link-light and plain text.

## 9. Tone and brand

- How should the sender sound? (formal to casual, expert to peer, dry to playful)
- Words or phrases the brand always uses, or never uses.
- A sample of writing to match: a past email, the founder's voice, a document.
- Formatting rules the brand holds. The author's defaults are no em dashes, short paragraphs, active voice and one CTA; the brand's own rules win.

## 10. Current state and benchmarks

- What outreach have you run before, and what happened? (reply rate, positive replies, meetings)
- Best and worst messages so far - paste them if you have them.
- What is a realistic target for this campaign?
- How will success be measured, and where does that data live?

## 11. Assets and access

- Links: website, best case study or one-pager, pricing, demo.
- Is the lead list ready, or does it still need building?
- Who approves copy, and how fast, so the cadence does not stall on review?

What I learned:

**Both relationships matter** - Happenstance's current guide distinguishes observed relationships from inferred shared backgrounds. This supports displaying each edge separately; it does not prove a numeric success rate.

**Activity is evidence, not certainty** - Affinity describes recency and frequency as inputs and its API cautions that interactions are only an estimate of relationship strength. Treat reciprocity, shared-team context and willingness as evidence to verify, not automatic additions to a score.

**Recent community evidence is insufficient for weight tuning** - The relevant material was mostly first-party descriptions. No supported recent benchmark established a better coefficient set. Retrieved unrelated items and promotional posts were excluded from factor conclusions. No qualifying top-community-comment block was returned, so no community quotations are invented.

KEY PATTERNS from the research:
1. Validate requester-to-connector and connector-to-target independently - per Happenstance.
2. Distinguish co-employment inference from an observed relationship - per Happenstance.
3. Evaluate dated reciprocal interactions rather than profile counts - per Affinity.
4. Require willingness and a specific reason before an ask - per Happenstance.

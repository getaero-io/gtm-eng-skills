# Resolve hierarchy

**Input:** starting identities, account unit, relationship types, effective date, jurisdictions, reference lists, and approved research limits.

1. Resolve each starting identity.
2. Check supplied domains through DNS, MX, website, and legal pages.
3. Record failures. Official registry evidence can still establish identity without a working website.
4. Use the hierarchy-discovery source matrix. Bound depth, records, requests, time, and cost before expansion.
5. Find parents, children, units, brands, and locations.
6. Keep unsupported candidates and unvisited work unresolved.
7. Check source lists, later transactions, and contrary evidence.
8. Classify each relationship by type, direction, and effective date.
9. Validate paths and prohibited cycles.
10. Infer an ultimate parent only through a complete verified path of one relationship type.
11. Propose CRM mappings.
12. Preserve buying units, routing, and sales coverage.
13. Retain unsupported types and dates outside native parent fields.

**Output:** dated graph, transaction evidence, source coverage, mapping proposals, unresolved work, and `hierarchy` receipts.

No bundled Play performs this discovery. Use verified research capabilities under the safety rule.

Check LEIs and accounting-consolidation relationships through the public [GLEIF API](https://api.gleif.org/api/v1/lei-records). Keep direct and ultimate parents separate. Reporting exceptions and absent records do not prove independence. For bulk work, use the [GLEIF Golden Copy](https://www.gleif.org/en/lei-data/gleif-golden-copy/download-the-golden-copy) only within the approved download and storage scope.

**Complete when:** every required source check and candidate has a result or an unresolved reason. Report recall only against a complete frozen reference list. Use proven distinct entities and parent-child links to prevent incorrect duplicate groups.
